The Problem

Your inbox didn't get messy overnight. It got messy one "10% off" signup at a time — until one day you're staring at 15,000 unread emails and Gmail's native unsubscribe button feels like bailing out a sinking ship with a teaspoon.

02

The Solution

The Gmail Cleaner finds your Heavy Hitters — the senders responsible for the bulk of your clutter — and lets you wipe their entire history in one shot. Batch delete, block future emails, then revoke access and walk away. What used to take an afternoon takes minutes.

03

How I Built It

I built this as a focused, single-purpose tool: fast to use, nothing stored, nothing lingering. I chose Streamlit to keep the UI lightweight and shippable quickly — the right tool for a utility that needed to just work. For ranking senders by volume, I used a Count-Min Sketch, a probabilistic data structure that trades a small margin of error for significant speed gains. For a cleanup tool, a close estimate is exactly what you need — you don't need perfect math to know who your top spammer is.

04

FAQ

Security was the first design decision, not an afterthought. Because this is a custom tool rather than a mass-market product, Google flags it as "unverified" — but here's what that actually means in practice: your emails are never stored, your data never touches a third-party database, and the app acts as a direct bridge to the Gmail API only for the duration of your session. When you're done, hit Revoke Access and the connection is completely severed.
Very close, but intentionally not perfect. The Count-Min Sketch approach prioritises speed — scanning thousands of email headers in seconds rather than minutes. The ranking might shift slightly during a live scan, but it will always surface the senders responsible for the bulk of your clutter. That's all you need to make the call.

Delete moves all emails from that sender to your Gmail Trash. They're not gone yet — you have 30 days to review or undo from your Trash folder.

Block creates a Gmail Filter in your actual Gmail settings, routing future emails from that sender straight to Trash. You can edit or remove these filters anytime inside Gmail.

Ignore is your whitelist. If your top Heavy Hitter is someone you actually want to keep — say, a family member you exchange a lot of email with — you can ignore them to remove them from the list for that session, so you can focus on the senders you actually want to act on. Ignored senders appear in a separate table below the Heavy Hitters list so you always have visibility. Changed your mind? You can add them back at any time.

Reset Data clears the current scan so you can start fresh — useful if your session feels stale or you want to re-scan after taking action.

For best results: scan your inbox → ignore anyone you want to keep → identify your top offenders → delete their history → block the ones you never want to hear from again → revoke access. Spring cleaning, done.
